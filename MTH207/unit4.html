About videos

Watching is the easiest thing to do and the least effective. A video you follow along with feels like understanding; the feeling does not survive contact with a blank page. These are here for when you want a second explanation or the idea behind a method — the problems are still where the learning happens.

3Blue1Brown, Essence of Calculus is listed against particular sections above. It shows you what a derivative or an integral is, with pictures, and never how to grind one out. That is exactly what it is good for. Most sections have no video listed, which is not an oversight.

blackpenredpen (bprp calculus basics) works enormous numbers of problems, and you will find him whether or not I link him, so here he is. Two honest cautions. First, watching a problem being solved is not solving one, and the two feel identical while you are doing it — go to him after you have attempted the problem yourself. Second, for “I am stuck on step 3”, a written solution beats a video every time: you can scan Paul's to step 3 in ten seconds, where a video makes you scrub, guess, and usually watch the whole thing. His own channel describes itself as being for people who “understand the theory but need to build computational fluency” — by his own account, not a first stop.
