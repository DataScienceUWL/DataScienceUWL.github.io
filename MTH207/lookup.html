MTH 207 — the quick reference

The slides will often say “the table of derivatives is here” rather than reprinting it. This is here. The tables below are the ones you will reach for most; everything further down is a link.

There is no formula sheet on exams. Everything on this page is fair game from memory. Three of these you have been asked to derive rather than recall — \(\frac{d}{dx}\sec x\), \(\frac{d}{dx}\tan^{-1}x\) and \(\frac{d}{dx}a^{x}\) — so knowing where they come from matters more than memorising them.

Differentiation rules

The four rules that combine the formulas above.
RuleStatement
Constant multiple\(\dfrac{d}{dx}\big[c\,f(x)\big] = c\,f\,'(x)\)
Sum and difference\(\dfrac{d}{dx}\big[f(x)\pm g(x)\big] = f\,'(x)\pm g\,'(x)\)
Product\(\dfrac{d}{dx}\big[f(x)g(x)\big] = f\,'(x)g(x)+f(x)g\,'(x)\)
Quotient\(\dfrac{d}{dx}\left[\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}\right] = \dfrac{f\,'(x)g(x)-f(x)g\,'(x)}{\big[g(x)\big]^{2}}\)
Chain\(\dfrac{d}{dx}\Big[f\big(g(x)\big)\Big] = f\,'\big(g(x)\big)\cdot g\,'(x)\)

Trigonometric identities

The identities this course actually uses.
NameIdentity
Definitions\(\tan\theta=\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta},\quad \sec\theta=\dfrac{1}{\cos\theta},\quad \csc\theta=\dfrac{1}{\sin\theta},\quad \cot\theta=\dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}\)
Pythagorean\(\sin^{2}\theta+\cos^{2}\theta=1\)
Pythagorean\(1+\tan^{2}\theta=\sec^{2}\theta\)
Pythagorean\(1+\cot^{2}\theta=\csc^{2}\theta\)
Double angle\(\sin 2\theta = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta\)
Double angle\(\cos 2\theta = \cos^{2}\theta-\sin^{2}\theta = 2\cos^{2}\theta-1 = 1-2\sin^{2}\theta\)

Limit laws

Throughout, suppose \(\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L\) and \(\lim_{x\to a}g(x)=M\), both existing.
LawStatement
Sum and difference\(\lim_{x\to a}\big[f(x)\pm g(x)\big]=L\pm M\)
Constant multiple\(\lim_{x\to a}\big[c\,f(x)\big]=cL\)
Product\(\lim_{x\to a}\big[f(x)g(x)\big]=LM\)
Quotient\(\lim_{x\to a}\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}=\dfrac{L}{M}\quad (M\neq 0)\)
Power\(\lim_{x\to a}\big[f(x)\big]^{n}=L^{n}\)
Root\(\lim_{x\to a}\sqrt[n]{f(x)}=\sqrt[n]{L}\)
Squeeze\(\text{If } f(x)\le g(x)\le h(x) \text{ near } a \text{ and } \lim_{x\to a}f=\lim_{x\to a}h=L,\text{ then }\lim_{x\to a}g=L\)
