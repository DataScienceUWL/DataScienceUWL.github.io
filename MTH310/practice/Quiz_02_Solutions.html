Problem 1: Line of Intersection of Two Planes

Find a vector form of the line of intersection of the planes:

\[3x - 2y + z = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad 2x + y - 3z = 3\]

Solution:

We want the form \(\vec{r} = \vec{r}_0 + t\vec{v}\) where \(\vec{r}_0\) is a point on the line and \(\vec{v}\) is the direction vector.

Step 1: Find a point on the line of intersection.

Set \(z = 0\) and solve the system:

\[3x - 2y = 1\]

\[2x + y = 3\]

Multiply the second equation by 2 and add:

\[3x - 2y + 4x + 2y = 1 + 6\]

\[7x = 7 \implies x = 1\]

Substitute back: \(2(1) + y = 3 \implies y = 1\)

So \(\vec{r}_0 = \langle 1, 1, 0 \rangle\) is a point on the line.

Step 2: Find the direction vector.

The direction vector is perpendicular to both normal vectors, so we compute \(\vec{n}_1 \times \vec{n}_2\):

\[\vec{n}_1 = \langle 3, -2, 1 \rangle, \quad \vec{n}_2 = \langle 2, 1, -3 \rangle\]

\[\vec{v} = \vec{n}_1 \times \vec{n}_2 = \begin{vmatrix} \vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\ 3 & -2 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & -3 \end{vmatrix}\]

\[= \vec{i}[(-2)(-3) - (1)(1)] - \vec{j}[(3)(-3) - (1)(2)] + \vec{k}[(3)(1) - (-2)(2)]\]

\[= \vec{i}(6 - 1) - \vec{j}(-9 - 2) + \vec{k}(3 + 4) = \langle 5, 11, 7 \rangle\]

Final Answer:

\[\boxed{\vec{r} = \langle 1, 1, 0 \rangle + t\langle 5, 11, 7 \rangle}\]

Note: Different equivalent answers are possible depending on the choice of point and direction.

Problem 2: Equation of a Plane Through Three Points

Find the scalar equation of the plane through the origin and points \(P(3, 2, 1)\) and \(Q(-1, 4, 2)\).

Solution:

We need a normal vector \(\vec{n}\) and a point \(\vec{r}_0\) on the plane.

Step 1: Choose a point.

Use the origin: \(\vec{r}_0 = \langle 0, 0, 0 \rangle\)

Step 2: Find two vectors parallel to the plane.

\[\vec{OP} = \langle 3, 2, 1 \rangle\]

\[\vec{OQ} = \langle -1, 4, 2 \rangle\]

Step 3: Find the normal vector using the cross product.

\[\vec{n} = \vec{OP} \times \vec{OQ} = \begin{vmatrix} \vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\ 3 & 2 & 1 \\ -1 & 4 & 2 \end{vmatrix}\]

\[= \vec{i}[(2)(2) - (1)(4)] - \vec{j}[(3)(2) - (1)(-1)] + \vec{k}[(3)(4) - (2)(-1)]\]

\[= \vec{i}(4 - 4) - \vec{j}(6 + 1) + \vec{k}(12 + 2) = \langle 0, -7, 14 \rangle\]

Step 4: Write the scalar equation.

Using \(\vec{n} \cdot \vec{r} = \vec{n} \cdot \vec{r}_0\):

\[\langle 0, -7, 14 \rangle \cdot \langle x, y, z \rangle = \langle 0, -7, 14 \rangle \cdot \langle 0, 0, 0 \rangle\]

\[0 \cdot x + (-7) \cdot y + 14 \cdot z = 0\]

Final Answer:

\[\boxed{-7y + 14z = 0}\]

Or equivalently: \(y - 2z = 0\) or \(y = 2z\)
