Problem 1: Volume Between Surfaces (Polar Coordinates)

Find the volume above the paraboloid \(z = x^2 + y^2\) and below the half-cone \(z = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\).

Cross-section in the r-z plane

Solution:

Setup: The surfaces intersect when \(x^2 + y^2 = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\), i.e., when \(r^2 = r\), giving \(r = 0\) or \(r = 1\). The region of integration is the unit disk.

In polar coordinates: \(z_{\text{top}} = r\) (cone) and \(z_{\text{bottom}} = r^2\) (paraboloid).

Step 1: Set up the integral.

\[V = \iint_D (z_{\text{top}} - z_{\text{bottom}}) \, dA = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 (r - r^2) \cdot r \, dr \, d\theta\]

Step 2: Evaluate the inner integral.

\[V = \int_0^{2\pi} d\theta \int_0^1 (r^2 - r^3) \, dr = 2\pi \left[\frac{r^3}{3} - \frac{r^4}{4}\right]_0^1\]

\[= 2\pi \left(\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{4}\right) = 2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{12} = \boxed{\frac{\pi}{6}}\]

Problem 2: Surface Area

Find the area of the part of the surface \(z = x + y^2\) that lies above the triangle with vertices \((0, 0)\), \((0, 1)\), and \((1, 1)\). One order of integration is much easier than the other.

Solution:

Setup: The surface area formula is:

\[S = \iint_T \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial z}{\partial y}\right)^2} \, dA\]

Step 1: Compute the partial derivatives.

\[\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = 1, \quad \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} = 2y\]

\[\sqrt{1 + 1 + 4y^2} = \sqrt{2 + 4y^2}\]

Step 2: Set up with the easier order (integrate \(x\) first).

The triangle: \(0 \le y \le 1\), \(0 \le x \le y\).

\[S = \int_0^1 \int_0^y \sqrt{2 + 4y^2} \, dx \, dy = \int_0^1 \sqrt{2 + 4y^2} \cdot y \, dy\]

Step 3: Use substitution \(u = 2 + 4y^2\), \(du = 8y \, dy\).

When \(y = 0\): \(u = 2\). When \(y = 1\): \(u = 6\).

\[S = \frac{1}{8} \int_2^6 \sqrt{u} \, du = \frac{1}{8} \cdot \frac{2}{3} u^{3/2} \Big|_2^6\]

\[= \frac{1}{12} \left(6^{3/2} - 2^{3/2}\right) = \frac{1}{12} \left(6\sqrt{6} - 2\sqrt{2}\right)\]

Final Answer:

\[= \boxed{\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{12}(3\sqrt{3} - 1) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{6}(3\sqrt{3} - 1)}\]

Problem 3: Area in Polar Coordinates (Four-Leaved Rose)

Find the total area inside the four loops of the quadrifolium or "four-leaved rose" \(r = 6\cos 2\theta\).

Solution:

Setup: One petal is traced for \(0 \le \theta \le \frac{\pi}{4}\). By symmetry, multiply by 8 (or use 4 petals times 2 halves each).

\[A = 8 \int_0^{\pi/4} \int_0^{6\cos 2\theta} r \, dr \, d\theta\]

Step 1: Evaluate the inner integral.

\[A = 8 \int_0^{\pi/4} \frac{1}{2} r^2 \Big|_0^{6\cos 2\theta} d\theta = 4 \int_0^{\pi/4} 36\cos^2 2\theta \, d\theta\]

\[= 144 \int_0^{\pi/4} \cos^2 2\theta \, d\theta\]

Step 2: Use the identity \(\cos^2 u = \frac{1 + \cos 2u}{2}\).

\[= 144 \int_0^{\pi/4} \frac{1 + \cos 4\theta}{2} \, d\theta = 72 \int_0^{\pi/4} (1 + \cos 4\theta) \, d\theta\]

Step 3: Evaluate.

\[= 72 \left[\theta + \frac{\sin 4\theta}{4}\right]_0^{\pi/4} = 72 \left(\frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\sin \pi}{4} - 0\right)\]

\[= 72 \cdot \frac{\pi}{4} = \boxed{18\pi}\]
