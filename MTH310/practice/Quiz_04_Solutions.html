Problem 1: Proving a Limit Does Not Exist

Prove that the limit \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y) \to (0,0)} \frac{xy^2}{x^2 + y^4}\) does not exist. At least one of the paths to approach the origin will need to be different than a line.

Solution:

To show the limit does not exist, we find two different paths that give different limits.

Path 1: Along \(x = 0\) (the y-axis)

\[\lim_{y \to 0} \frac{(0) \cdot y^2}{0^2 + y^4} = \lim_{y \to 0} \frac{0}{y^4} = \lim_{y \to 0} 0 = \boxed{0}\]

Path 2: Along \(x = y^2\) (a parabola)

\[\lim_{y \to 0} \frac{(y^2) \cdot y^2}{(y^2)^2 + y^4} = \lim_{y \to 0} \frac{y^4}{y^4 + y^4} = \lim_{y \to 0} \frac{y^4}{2y^4} = \lim_{y \to 0} \frac{1}{2} = \boxed{\frac{1}{2}}\]

Conclusion: Since we get different limits along different paths (\(0 \neq \frac{1}{2}\)), the limit

\[\lim_{(x,y) \to (0,0)} \frac{xy^2}{x^2 + y^4} \text{ does not exist (DNE)}\]

Problem 2: Implicit Partial Differentiation

Find and solve for \(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial x}\) if \(e^z = xyz\).

Solution:

Use implicit differentiation with respect to \(x\) (treating \(y\) as a constant):

Step 1: Differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\):

\[\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(e^z\right) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(xyz)\]

Step 2: Apply the chain rule on the left and product rule on the right:

\[e^z \cdot \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = xy \cdot \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} + yz\]

Step 3: Solve for \(\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial x}\):

\[e^z \cdot \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} - xy \cdot \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = yz\]

\[\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\left(e^z - xy\right) = yz\]

\[\boxed{\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = \frac{yz}{e^z - xy}}\]

Alternative forms: Using the original equation \(e^z = xyz\), we can substitute:

\[\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = \frac{yz}{xyz - xy} = \frac{yz}{xy(z - 1)} = \frac{z}{x(z - 1)}\]

All of these equivalent forms are valid answers.
