Problem 1: Equation of a Sphere

Find the equation of the sphere that passes through the origin and has center \((1, 2, 3)\).

Solution:

The radius of the sphere is the distance from the center \(P(1, 2, 3)\) to the origin \(O(0, 0, 0)\):

\[D(O, P) = \sqrt{1^2 + 2^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 9} = \sqrt{14}\]

The equation of a sphere with center \((h, k, l)\) and radius \(r\) is:

\[(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 + (z - l)^2 = r^2\]

Substituting our values:

\[\boxed{(x - 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 + (z - 3)^2 = 14}\]

Problem 2: Describing a Region

Describe the region described by the inequalities \(9 \leq y^2 + z^2 \leq 25\).

Solution:

The inequality \(y^2 + z^2 \leq 25\) describes points inside or on a cylinder of radius 5 centered on the \(x\)-axis.

The inequality \(y^2 + z^2 \geq 9\) describes points outside or on a cylinder of radius 3 centered on the \(x\)-axis.

Combined, \(9 \leq y^2 + z^2 \leq 25\) describes:

A solid circular cylinder around the \(x\)-axis with radius 5, but with a core cylinder of radius 3 removed (a hollow cylindrical shell or "pipe").

Cross-section in the \(yz\)-plane (looking along \(x\)-axis)

Problem 3: Resultant Force

Find the magnitude of the resultant force and the angle it makes with the positive \(x\)-axis.

Note: \(\cos 30° = \sin 60° = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\) and \(\cos 60° = \sin 30° = \frac{1}{2}\). It's OK to leave square roots in your answers.

Solution:

Step 1: Express each force as a vector.

Force \(\vec{b}\) has magnitude 200 N at 60° from the positive \(x\)-axis:

\[\vec{b} = 200\langle \cos 60°, \sin 60° \rangle = 200\left\langle \frac{1}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right\rangle = \langle 100, 100\sqrt{3} \rangle\]

Force \(\vec{a}\) has magnitude 300 N pointing in the negative \(x\)-direction:

\[\vec{a} = 300\langle -1, 0 \rangle = \langle -300, 0 \rangle\]

Step 2: Find the resultant force \(\vec{F} = \vec{a} + \vec{b}\):

\[\vec{F} = \langle -300, 0 \rangle + \langle 100, 100\sqrt{3} \rangle = \langle -200, 100\sqrt{3} \rangle\]

Step 3: Find the magnitude:

\[\|\vec{F}\| = \sqrt{(-200)^2 + (100\sqrt{3})^2} = \sqrt{40000 + 30000} = \sqrt{70000} = \boxed{100\sqrt{7} \text{ N}}\]

Step 4: Find the angle with the positive \(x\)-axis:

Using the dot product formula:

\[\cos\theta = \frac{\vec{F} \cdot \vec{i}}{\|\vec{F}\| \|\vec{i}\|} = \frac{-200}{100\sqrt{7}} = \frac{-2}{\sqrt{7}}\]

\[\theta = \cos^{-1}\left(\frac{-2}{\sqrt{7}}\right) \approx \boxed{139.1°}\]

Alternative method using tangent:

\[\tan\alpha = \frac{100\sqrt{3}}{200} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \implies \alpha = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) \approx 40.9°\]

Since the vector is in the second quadrant (negative \(x\), positive \(y\)):

\[\theta = 180° - 40.9° = 139.1°\]
