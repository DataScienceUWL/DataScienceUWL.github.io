Problem 1: Surface Integral on a Helicoid

Setup and evaluate \(\displaystyle\iint_S y \, dS\), where \(S\) is the helicoid (spiral ramp) with vector equation \(\mathbf{r}(u, v) = \langle u\cos v, u\sin v, v \rangle\), \(0 \le u \le 1\), \(0 \le v \le 2\pi\).

Solution:

Step 1: Find \(\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v\).

\[\mathbf{r}_u = \langle \cos v, \sin v, 0 \rangle\]

\[\mathbf{r}_v = \langle -u\sin v, u\cos v, 1 \rangle\]

\[\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ \cos v & \sin v & 0 \\ -u\sin v & u\cos v & 1 \end{vmatrix}\]

\[= \langle \sin v - 0, -(cos v - 0), u\cos^2 v + u\sin^2 v \rangle = \langle \sin v, -\cos v, u \rangle\]

Step 2: Find \(|\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v|\).

\[|\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v| = \sqrt{\sin^2 v + \cos^2 v + u^2} = \sqrt{1 + u^2}\]

Step 3: Set up and evaluate the integral.

On the surface, \(y = u\sin v\).

\[\iint_S y \, dS = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 (u\sin v) \sqrt{1 + u^2} \, du \, dv\]

\[= \int_0^1 u\sqrt{1 + u^2} \, du \cdot \int_0^{2\pi} \sin v \, dv\]

Step 4: The second integral is zero!

\[\int_0^{2\pi} \sin v \, dv = -\cos v \Big|_0^{2\pi} = -1 - (-1) = 0\]

Final Answer:

\[\boxed{\iint_S y \, dS = 0}\]

Problem 2: Surface Integral over z = g(x,y)

Setup and evaluate \(\displaystyle\iint_S x \, dS\) where \(S\) is the surface \(z = x^2 + y\), \(0 \le x \le 2\), \(0 \le y \le 1\).

Solution:

Setup: For a surface \(z = g(x, y)\):

\[\iint_S f(x, y, z) \, dS = \iint_D f(x, y, g(x,y)) \sqrt{1 + g_x^2 + g_y^2} \, dA\]

Step 1: Compute the partial derivatives.

\[g_x = 2x, \quad g_y = 1\]

\[\sqrt{1 + (2x)^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2 + 4x^2}\]

Step 2: Set up the integral.

\[\iint_S x \, dS = \int_0^1 \int_0^2 x\sqrt{2 + 4x^2} \, dx \, dy\]

\[= \int_0^1 dy \cdot \int_0^2 x\sqrt{2 + 4x^2} \, dx = 1 \cdot \int_0^2 x\sqrt{2 + 4x^2} \, dx\]

Step 3: Use substitution \(u = 2 + 4x^2\), \(du = 8x \, dx\).

When \(x = 0\): \(u = 2\). When \(x = 2\): \(u = 18\).

\[= \frac{1}{8} \int_2^{18} \sqrt{u} \, du = \frac{1}{8} \cdot \frac{2}{3} u^{3/2} \Big|_2^{18}\]

\[= \frac{1}{12} \left(18^{3/2} - 2^{3/2}\right)\]

Step 4: Simplify.

\[18^{3/2} = (9 \cdot 2)^{3/2} = 27 \cdot 2\sqrt{2} = 54\sqrt{2}\]

\[2^{3/2} = 2\sqrt{2}\]

\[= \frac{1}{12}(54\sqrt{2} - 2\sqrt{2}) = \frac{52\sqrt{2}}{12} = \boxed{\frac{13\sqrt{2}}{3}}\]

Problem 3: Surface Integral of Vector Field (Flux) - Paraboloid

Setup and evaluate \(\displaystyle\iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S}\) where \(\mathbf{F} = \langle xy, y, z \rangle\) and \(S\) is the part of the paraboloid \(z = 4 - x^2 - y^2\) that lies above the square \(0 \le x \le 1\), \(0 \le y \le 1\).

Solution:

Setup: For a surface \(z = g(x, y)\) with upward orientation:

\[\iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iint_D \mathbf{F} \cdot \langle -g_x, -g_y, 1 \rangle \, dA\]

Step 1: Find the partial derivatives of \(g(x,y) = 4 - x^2 - y^2\).

\[g_x = -2x, \quad g_y = -2y\]

\[\langle -g_x, -g_y, 1 \rangle = \langle 2x, 2y, 1 \rangle\]

Step 2: Write \(\mathbf{F}\) on the surface.

On \(S\): \(z = 4 - x^2 - y^2\), so \(\mathbf{F} = \langle xy, y, 4 - x^2 - y^2 \rangle\).

Step 3: Compute the dot product.

\[\mathbf{F} \cdot \langle 2x, 2y, 1 \rangle = 2x^2y + 2y^2 + 4 - x^2 - y^2\]

Step 4: Integrate.

\[\iint_D (2x^2y + 2y^2 + 4 - x^2 - y^2) \, dA = \int_0^1 \int_0^1 (2x^2y + y^2 - x^2 + 4) \, dy \, dx\]

\[= \int_0^1 \left[x^2y^2 + \frac{y^3}{3} - x^2y + 4y\right]_0^1 dx\]

\[= \int_0^1 \left(x^2 + \frac{1}{3} - x^2 + 4\right) dx = \int_0^1 \frac{13}{3} \, dx = \boxed{\frac{13}{3}}\]

Problem 4: Surface Integral of Vector Field (Flux) - Hemisphere

Setup and evaluate \(\displaystyle\iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S}\) where \(\mathbf{F} = \langle y, -x, 2z \rangle\) and \(S\) is the hemisphere \(x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 4\), \(z \ge 0\) oriented downward.

Solution:

Setup: Parameterize the hemisphere using spherical coordinates with \(\rho = 2\):

\[\mathbf{r}(\phi, \theta) = \langle 2\cos\theta\sin\phi, 2\sin\theta\sin\phi, 2\cos\phi \rangle\]

where \(0 \le \phi \le \frac{\pi}{2}\), \(0 \le \theta \le 2\pi\).

Step 1: Find \(\mathbf{r}_\phi \times \mathbf{r}_\theta\).

For outward orientation: \(\mathbf{r}_\phi \times \mathbf{r}_\theta = 4\sin\phi \langle \cos\theta\sin\phi, \sin\theta\sin\phi, \cos\phi \rangle\)

For downward (inward) orientation, we negate:

\[-(\mathbf{r}_\phi \times \mathbf{r}_\theta) = -4\sin\phi \langle \cos\theta\sin\phi, \sin\theta\sin\phi, \cos\phi \rangle\]

Step 2: Write \(\mathbf{F}\) on the surface.

\[\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}) = \langle 2\sin\theta\sin\phi, -2\cos\theta\sin\phi, 4\cos\phi \rangle\]

Step 3: Compute the dot product with the inward normal.

\[\mathbf{F} \cdot (-4\sin\phi \langle \cos\theta\sin\phi, \sin\theta\sin\phi, \cos\phi \rangle)\]

\[= -4\sin\phi(2\sin\theta\cos\theta\sin^2\phi - 2\cos\theta\sin\theta\sin^2\phi + 4\cos^2\phi)\]

The first two terms cancel: \(\sin\theta\cos\theta - \cos\theta\sin\theta = 0\).

\[= -4\sin\phi \cdot 4\cos^2\phi = -16\cos^2\phi\sin\phi\]

Step 4: Integrate.

\[\iint_S \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^{\pi/2} -16\cos^2\phi\sin\phi \, d\phi \, d\theta\]

\[= -16 \int_0^{2\pi} d\theta \cdot \int_0^{\pi/2} \cos^2\phi\sin\phi \, d\phi\]

Let \(u = \cos\phi\), \(du = -\sin\phi \, d\phi\):

\[\int_0^{\pi/2} \cos^2\phi\sin\phi \, d\phi = \int_1^0 u^2(-du) = \int_0^1 u^2 \, du = \frac{1}{3}\]

\[= -16 \cdot 2\pi \cdot \frac{1}{3} = \boxed{-\frac{32\pi}{3}}\]
