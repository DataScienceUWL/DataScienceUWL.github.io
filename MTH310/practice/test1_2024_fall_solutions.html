1. (8 pts) Find the work done by a force \(\vec{F} = \vec{i} + 5\vec{j} + 7\vec{k}\) that moves an object from the point \((3, 0, 5)\) to the point \((7, 5, 10)\) along a straight line. The distance is measured in meters and the force in Newtons.

Solution:

Work is calculated using the dot product: \(W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d}\)

First, find the displacement vector:

\[\vec{PQ} = \langle 7-3, 5-0, 10-5 \rangle = \langle 4, 5, 5 \rangle\]

Then compute the dot product:

\[W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{PQ} = \langle 1, 5, 7 \rangle \cdot \langle 4, 5, 5 \rangle\] \[= (1)(4) + (5)(5) + (7)(5) = 4 + 25 + 35 = 64 \text{ N} \cdot \text{m}\]

Answer: \(W = 64\) J

2. (10 pts) Find the area of the triangle formed by points \(P(1, 0, 0)\), \(Q(5, 4, 0)\), and \(R(0, 4, 1)\).

Solution:

The area of a triangle is half the magnitude of the cross product of two edge vectors.

Find vectors \(\vec{PQ}\) and \(\vec{PR}\):

\[\vec{PQ} = \langle 5-1, 4-0, 0-0 \rangle = \langle 4, 4, 0 \rangle\] \[\vec{PR} = \langle 0-1, 4-0, 1-0 \rangle = \langle -1, 4, 1 \rangle\]

Compute the cross product:

\[\vec{PQ} \times \vec{PR} = \begin{vmatrix} \vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\ 4 & 4 & 0 \\ -1 & 4 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = \langle 4, -4, 20 \rangle\]

Find the magnitude:

\[|\vec{PQ} \times \vec{PR}| = \sqrt{4^2 + (-4)^2 + 20^2} = \sqrt{16 + 16 + 400} = \sqrt{432}\]

Area of triangle:

\[A_\triangle = \frac{1}{2}|\vec{PQ} \times \vec{PR}| = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{432} \approx 10.4\]

Answer: \(A \approx 10.4\) square units (or \(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{432} = \frac{\sqrt{432}}{2}\))

3. (12 pts) Find an equation for the plane passing through the point \((1, -5, 2)\) and containing the \(x\)-axis.

Solution:

Since the plane contains the \(x\)-axis, it passes through the origin and any point on the \(x\)-axis.

Choose 3 points on the plane:

  • \(P(0, 0, 0)\) - the origin (on \(x\)-axis)
  • \(Q(1, 0, 0)\) - another point on \(x\)-axis
  • \(R(1, -5, 2)\) - the given point

Find vectors in the plane:

\[\vec{PQ} = \langle 1, 0, 0 \rangle\] \[\vec{PR} = \langle 1, -5, 2 \rangle\]

Find normal vector using cross product:

\[\vec{n} = \vec{PQ} \times \vec{PR} = \begin{vmatrix} \vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & -5 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = \langle 0, -2, -5 \rangle\]

Using point \(P(0,0,0)\) and normal \(\langle 0, -2, -5 \rangle\):

\[\vec{n} \cdot (\vec{r} - \vec{r}_0) = 0\] \[\langle 0, -2, -5 \rangle \cdot \langle x, y, z \rangle = \langle 0, -2, -5 \rangle \cdot \langle 0, 0, 0 \rangle\]

Answer: \(-2y - 5z = 0\) (or equivalently \(2y + 5z = 0\))

4. (10 pts) Find the point where the line \(\vec{r}(t) = \langle t-1, 1+2t, 3-t \rangle\) intersects the plane \(3x - y + 2z = 5\).

Solution:

From the parametric equations: \(x = t-1\), \(y = 1+2t\), \(z = 3-t\)

Substitute into the plane equation:

\[3(t-1) - (1+2t) + 2(3-t) = 5\] \[3t - 3 - 1 - 2t + 6 - 2t = 5\] \[-t + 2 = 5\] \[-t = 3\] \[t = -3\]

Find the coordinates:

\[x = -3 - 1 = -4\] \[y = 1 + 2(-3) = -5\] \[z = 3 - (-3) = 6\]

Check: \(3(-4) - (-5) + 2(6) = -12 + 5 + 12 = 5\) ✓

Answer: \((-4, -5, 6)\)

5. (8 pts) Matching Surfaces:

(a) Match each equation (in the table) with the graph of a surface. Enter O, if there is no match. The standard orientation of the axes applies in each graph.

Detailed description of the figure: The image shows a 3D coordinate system with positive x, y, and z axes labeled, followed by three surface graphs. Surface I appears as two separate bowl-shaped pieces opening away from each other along the x-axis, characteristic of a hyperboloid of two sheets. Surface II is a flat plane tilted in 3D space, intersecting all three coordinate axes. Surface III is a connected surface with a saddle shape, curving upward in one direction and downward in the perpendicular direction, characteristic of a hyperbolic paraboloid.
Equation Answer (O, I, II, III)
\(x + y^2 + z^2 = 1\) O
\(x + y + z = 1\) II
\(x + y^2 - z^2 = 1\) III
\(-x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1\) I

Solution (Justifications):

  • \(x + y^2 + z^2 = 1\): Rewrite as \(x = 1 - y^2 - z^2\). This is a paraboloid opening in the negative \(x\)-direction, but none shown → O
  • \(x + y + z = 1\): This is clearly a plane → II
  • \(x + y^2 - z^2 = 1\): Cross-sections are parabolas and hyperbolas. This is a hyperbolic paraboloid → III
  • \(-x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1\): For fixed \(x\), we get circles. For fixed \(y\) or \(z\), we get hyperbolas. This is a hyperboloid of one sheet → I
6. (8 pts) Sketch the curve of the vector function \(\vec{r}(t) = 3\sin t\, \vec{i} + 1\cos t\, \vec{j}\) and indicate the orientation of the curve.

Solution:

This is a circle stretched in the \(x\)-direction — an ellipse.

From \(x = 3\sin t\) and \(y = \cos t\):

\[\frac{x^2}{3^2} + \frac{y^2}{1^2} = \sin^2 t + \cos^2 t = 1\] \[\frac{x^2}{9} + y^2 = 1\]

This is an ellipse with semi-major axis 3 along the \(x\)-axis and semi-minor axis 1 along the \(y\)-axis.

Orientation: At \(t = 0\): \((x, y) = (0, 1)\). As \(t\) increases, \(x\) increases and \(y\) decreases. The curve traces clockwise.

7. (8 pts) Find a vector function that represents the curve of intersection of the two surfaces: \(x^2 + y^2 = 4\) and \(z = xy\).

Solution:

The first equation \(x^2 + y^2 = 4\) is a circle of radius 2. Parametrize it:

\[x = 2\cos t, \quad y = 2\sin t\]

Substitute into \(z = xy\):

\[z = (2\cos t)(2\sin t) = 4\cos t \sin t = 2\sin 2t\]

Answer:

\[\vec{r}(t) = \langle 2\cos t, 2\sin t, 4\cos t \sin t \rangle = \langle 2\cos t, 2\sin t, 2\sin 2t \rangle\]
8. (12 pts) The angle between intersecting curves is the angle between their tangent vectors. The curves \(\vec{r}_1(t) = \langle t, t^3, t^9 \rangle\) and \(\vec{r}_2(t) = \langle \sin t, \sin 5t, t \rangle\) intersect at the origin. Find their angle of intersection correct to the nearest degree.

Solution:

Find the tangent vectors at the origin (where \(t = 0\)):

\[\vec{r}_1'(t) = \langle 1, 3t^2, 9t^8 \rangle\] \[\vec{a} = \vec{r}_1'(0) = \langle 1, 0, 0 \rangle\] \[\vec{r}_2'(t) = \langle \cos t, 5\cos 5t, 1 \rangle\] \[\vec{b} = \vec{r}_2'(0) = \langle 1, 5, 1 \rangle\]

Use the dot product formula for angle:

\[\cos \theta = \frac{\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b}}{|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|} = \frac{\langle 1, 0, 0 \rangle \cdot \langle 1, 5, 1 \rangle}{|\langle 1, 0, 0 \rangle||\langle 1, 5, 1 \rangle|}\] \[= \frac{1}{\sqrt{1} \cdot \sqrt{1 + 25 + 1}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{27}}\] \[\theta = \cos^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{27}}\right) \approx 79°\]

Answer: \(\theta \approx 79°\)

9. (12 pts) A spaceship is following the trajectory \(\vec{r}(t) = \langle 3t, 5t^2, 4t^3 \rangle\) for \(t \leq 1\). At time \(t = 1\) it turns off its thrusters and thereafter follows the tangent line to the curve. Does the spaceship intersect the point \((12, 35, 40)\)? If so, how many seconds after it turns off its thrusters?

Solution:

At \(t = 1\), find position and velocity:

\[\vec{r}(1) = \langle 3, 5, 4 \rangle\] \[\vec{r}'(t) = \langle 3, 10t, 12t^2 \rangle\] \[\vec{r}'(1) = \langle 3, 10, 12 \rangle\]

The tangent line equation (where \(t\) now represents time after \(t = 1\)):

\[\vec{L}(t) = \langle 3, 5, 4 \rangle + t\langle 3, 10, 12 \rangle = \langle 3 + 3t, 5 + 10t, 4 + 12t \rangle\]

Set equal to target point \((12, 35, 40)\):

\[\langle 3 + 3t, 5 + 10t, 4 + 12t \rangle = \langle 12, 35, 40 \rangle\]

From each component:

  • \(3 + 3t = 12 \Rightarrow t = 3\)
  • \(5 + 10t = 35 \Rightarrow t = 3\)
  • \(4 + 12t = 40 \Rightarrow t = 3\)

All three equations give \(t = 3\). ✓

Answer: Yes, 3 seconds after turning off thrusters.

10. For the function \(f(x, y) = \sqrt{y - x^2}\):

(a) (4 pts) What is the domain of \(f\)?

Solution:

We need \(y - x^2 \geq 0\), which means \(y \geq x^2\).

Domain: \(\{(x, y) \mid y \geq x^2\}\)

This is the region on and above the parabola \(y = x^2\).

(b) (8 pts) Carefully sketch a contour map showing level curves corresponding to \(z = 0, z = 1, z = 2, z = 3\).

Solution:

For level curve \(z = k\): \(\sqrt{y - x^2} = k\)

Squaring: \(y - x^2 = k^2\), so \(y = x^2 + k^2\)

These are upward-opening parabolas shifted up by \(k^2\):

  • \(k = 0\): \(y = x^2\) (vertex at origin)
  • \(k = 1\): \(y = x^2 + 1\) (vertex at \((0, 1)\))
  • \(k = 2\): \(y = x^2 + 4\) (vertex at \((0, 4)\))
  • \(k = 3\): \(y = x^2 + 9\) (vertex at \((0, 9)\))

The contour map shows nested upward-opening parabolas, with larger \(k\) values corresponding to parabolas shifted higher up.
