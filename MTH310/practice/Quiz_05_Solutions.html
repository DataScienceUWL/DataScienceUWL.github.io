Problem 1: Linear Approximation

Find the linear approximation of \(f(x, y) = 1 - xy\cos(\pi y)\) at \((1, 1)\) and use it to approximate \(f(1.02, 0.97)\).

Solution:

Step 1: Find the partial derivatives.

\[f_x = -y\cos(\pi y)\]

\[f_y = -x\left(y \cdot \pi \sin(\pi y) + \cos(\pi y)\right)\]

Step 2: Evaluate at \((1, 1)\).

\[f_x(1, 1) = -1 \cdot \cos(\pi) = -1 \cdot (-1) = 1\]

\[f_y(1, 1) = -1\left(1 \cdot \pi \cdot \sin(\pi) + \cos(\pi)\right) = -1(0 + (-1)) = 1\]

\[f(1, 1) = 1 - 1 \cdot 1 \cdot \cos(\pi) = 1 - (-1) = 2\]

Step 3: Write the linear approximation.

\[L(x, y) = f(1, 1) + f_x(1, 1)(x - 1) + f_y(1, 1)(y - 1)\]

\[L(x, y) = 2 + 1(x - 1) + 1(y - 1)\]

Step 4: Approximate \(f(1.02, 0.97)\).

\[f(1.02, 0.97) \approx L(1.02, 0.97) = 2 + (1.02 - 1) + (0.97 - 1)\]

\[= 2 + 0.02 - 0.03 = \boxed{1.99}\]

Exact value: \(f(1.02, 0.97) \approx 1.985009...\)

Problem 2: Chain Rule

\(w = x^2 + y^2 + z^2\), where \(x = st\), \(y = s\cos t\), \(z = s\sin t\).

Find \(\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial s}\) at \((s, t) = (4, 0)\). Use the chain rule.

Solution:

Step 1: Find the values at \((s, t) = (4, 0)\).

\[x = 4 \cdot 0 = 0, \quad y = 4\cos(0) = 4, \quad z = 4\sin(0) = 0\]

Step 2: Apply the chain rule.

\[\frac{\partial w}{\partial s} = \frac{\partial w}{\partial x}\frac{\partial x}{\partial s} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial y}\frac{\partial y}{\partial s} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial z}\frac{\partial z}{\partial s}\]

\[= 2x \cdot t + 2y \cdot \cos t + 2z \cdot \sin t\]

Step 3: Evaluate at \((s, t) = (4, 0)\).

\[\frac{\partial w}{\partial s}\bigg|_{s=4, t=0} = 2(0)(0) + 2(4)\cos(0) + 2(0)\sin(0)\]

\[= 0 + 8 \cdot 1 + 0 = \boxed{8}\]

Problem 3: Tangent Plane

For the surface \(y = x^2 - z^2\) at the point \((4, 7, 3)\), find the equation of the tangent plane.

Solution:

Step 1: Rewrite as \(F(x, y, z) = x^2 - z^2 - y = 0\).

Step 2: Find the gradient (normal vector).

\[\nabla F = \langle 2x, -1, -2z \rangle\]

\[\vec{n} = \nabla F(4, 7, 3) = \langle 2(4), -1, -2(3) \rangle = \langle 8, -1, -6 \rangle\]

Step 3: Write the tangent plane equation.

\[\vec{n} \cdot (\vec{r} - \vec{r}_0) = 0\]

\[\langle 8, -1, -6 \rangle \cdot \langle x - 4, y - 7, z - 3 \rangle = 0\]

\[8(x - 4) - 1(y - 7) - 6(z - 3) = 0\]

\[8x - 32 - y + 7 - 6z + 18 = 0\]

\[\boxed{8x - y - 6z = 7}\]

Problem 4: Directional Derivative and Gradient

Consider the function \(f(x, y) = \dfrac{x}{x^2 + y^2}\).

  • Find the directional derivative at \((1, 2)\) moving toward \((-2, 6)\).
  • Find the maximum rate of change of \(f\) at the point \((1, 2)\) and the direction in which it occurs.

Solution:

Step 1: Find the partial derivatives.

\[f_x = \frac{(x^2 + y^2)(1) - x(2x)}{(x^2 + y^2)^2} = \frac{y^2 - x^2}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}\]

\[f_y = \frac{-x(2y)}{(x^2 + y^2)^2} = \frac{-2xy}{(x^2 + y^2)^2}\]

Step 2: Evaluate at \((1, 2)\).

\[f_x(1, 2) = \frac{4 - 1}{25} = \frac{3}{25}\]

\[f_y(1, 2) = \frac{-4}{25}\]

\[\nabla f(1, 2) = \left\langle \frac{3}{25}, \frac{-4}{25} \right\rangle\]

Part (a): Directional Derivative

Direction from \(P(1, 2)\) to \(Q(-2, 6)\):

\[\vec{PQ} = \langle -3, 4 \rangle, \quad \|\vec{PQ}\| = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5\]

\[\vec{u} = \left\langle \frac{-3}{5}, \frac{4}{5} \right\rangle\]

\[D_{\vec{u}}f(1, 2) = \nabla f(1, 2) \cdot \vec{u} = \left\langle \frac{3}{25}, \frac{-4}{25} \right\rangle \cdot \left\langle \frac{-3}{5}, \frac{4}{5} \right\rangle\]

\[= \frac{-9}{125} + \frac{-16}{125} = \frac{-25}{125} = \boxed{-\frac{1}{5}}\]

Part (b): Maximum Rate of Change

Maximum rate of change \(= \|\nabla f(1, 2)\|\):

\[\|\nabla f(1, 2)\| = \sqrt{\left(\frac{3}{25}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{-4}{25}\right)^2} = \sqrt{\frac{9 + 16}{625}} = \sqrt{\frac{25}{625}} = \frac{5}{25} = \boxed{\frac{1}{5}}\]

Direction of maximum increase: \(\nabla f(1, 2) = \left\langle \frac{3}{25}, \frac{-4}{25} \right\rangle\) (or any positive scalar multiple)

Problem 5: Differentials and Error Estimation

Three positive numbers, each less than 50, are rounded to the first decimal place (e.g., 14.078 becomes 14.1) and then multiplied together. Use differentials to estimate the maximum possible error in the computed product that might result from rounding.

Solution:

Setup: Let \(w = xyz\) where \(0 < x, y, z < 50\).

Rounding to the first decimal place introduces a maximum error of \(\pm 0.05\) for each number.

So \(|dx|, |dy|, |dz| \leq 0.05\).

Step 1: Find the differential.

\[dw = w_x \, dx + w_y \, dy + w_z \, dz\]

\[dw = yz \, dx + xz \, dy + xy \, dz\]

Step 2: Maximize the error.

The maximum error occurs when all variables are at their maximum (\(x = y = z = 50\)) and all errors add up:

\[|dw| \leq |yz||dx| + |xz||dy| + |xy||dz|\]

\[|dw| \leq 50^2(0.05) + 50^2(0.05) + 50^2(0.05)\]

\[= 3 \cdot 50^2 \cdot 0.05 = 3 \cdot 2500 \cdot 0.05 = \boxed{375}\]
