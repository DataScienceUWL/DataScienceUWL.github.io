Question 1 [4 pts]

Evaluate the integral \(\iiint_E z\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\, dV\), where \(E\) is the solid inside the cylinder \(x^2 + y^2 = 1\), between \(z = 0\) and the cone \(z = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}\).

Convert to cylindrical: \(\sqrt{x^2+y^2} = r\), \(dV = r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta\).

The region: \(0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi\), \(0 \leq r \leq 1\), \(0 \leq z \leq r\).

The integrand becomes \(z \cdot r\), so: \[\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1\int_0^r z \cdot r \cdot r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1\int_0^r z\, r^2\,dz\,dr\,d\theta\]

Inner integral (\(dz\)): \[\int_0^r z\, r^2\,dz = r^2 \cdot \frac{z^2}{2}\Big|_0^r = \frac{r^4}{2}\]

Middle integral (\(dr\)): \[\int_0^1 \frac{r^4}{2}\,dr = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{r^5}{5}\Big|_0^1 = \frac{1}{10}\]

Outer integral (\(d\theta\)): \[\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{1}{10}\,d\theta = \boxed{\frac{\pi}{5}}\]

Question 2 [4 pts]

Evaluate the integral \(\iiint_E z\, dV\), where \(E\) is the region inside the sphere \(x^2+y^2+z^2 = 4\) and above the cone \(\phi = \pi/3\).

Convert to spherical: \(z = \rho\cos\phi\), \(dV = \rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta\).

The region: \(0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi\), \(0 \leq \phi \leq \pi/3\), \(0 \leq \rho \leq 2\).

\[\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi/3}\int_0^2 \rho\cos\phi \cdot \rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi/3}\int_0^2 \rho^3\cos\phi\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta\]

Inner integral (\(d\rho\)): \[\int_0^2 \rho^3\,d\rho = \frac{\rho^4}{4}\Big|_0^2 = 4\]

Middle integral (\(d\phi\)): Let \(u = \sin\phi\), \(du = \cos\phi\,d\phi\): \[\int_0^{\pi/3} \cos\phi\sin\phi\,d\phi = \frac{\sin^2\phi}{2}\Big|_0^{\pi/3} = \frac{(\sqrt{3}/2)^2}{2} = \frac{3}{8}\]

Outer integral (\(d\theta\)): \[\int_0^{2\pi} 4 \cdot \frac{3}{8}\,d\theta = \frac{3}{2}\cdot 2\pi = \boxed{3\pi}\]

Question 3 [3 pts]

(a) (2 pts) Compute \(\nabla f\) for \(f(x,y) = xe^y + y^2\).

\[\nabla f = \langle f_x, f_y \rangle = \langle e^y,\; xe^y + 2y \rangle\]

(b) (1 pts) Using part (a), explain why the vector field \(\mathbf{F}(x,y) = \langle e^y,\; xe^y + 2y \rangle\) is conservative.

Since \(\nabla f = \langle e^y, xe^y + 2y \rangle = \mathbf{F}\), the field \(\mathbf{F}\) is a gradient field. By definition, a vector field that equals \(\nabla f\) for some function \(f\) is conservative.

Question 4 [4 pts]

Evaluate the line integral \(\int_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}\), where \(\mathbf{F}(x,y) = \langle x+y,\; y \rangle\) and \(C\) is the line segment from \((1,0)\) to \((3,2)\).

Parameterize: \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle 1, 0 \rangle + t\langle 2, 2 \rangle = \langle 1+2t,\; 2t \rangle\), \(0 \leq t \leq 1\).

\(\mathbf{r}'(t) = \langle 2, 2 \rangle\).

Compute \(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t))\): \[\mathbf{F} = \langle (1+2t) + 2t,\; 2t \rangle = \langle 1+4t,\; 2t \rangle\]

Dot product: \[\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{r}'(t) = (1+4t)(2) + (2t)(2) = 2 + 8t + 4t = 2 + 12t\]

Integrate: \[\int_0^1 (2 + 12t)\,dt = \left[2t + 6t^2\right]_0^1 = 2 + 6 = \boxed{8}\]
