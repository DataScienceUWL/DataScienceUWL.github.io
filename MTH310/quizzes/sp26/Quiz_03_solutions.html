Question 1. 9 pts

Consider the surface \(z = 4 - x^2 - 4y^2\).

  1. Find the horizontal traces (\(z = k\)). For what values of \(k\) do real traces exist?

    Set \(z = k\): then \(k = 4 - x^2 - 4y^2\), or equivalently \[x^2 + 4y^2 = 4 - k \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{x^2}{4-k} + \frac{y^2}{(4-k)/4} = 1.\]

    • \(k < 4\): ellipses (wider in \(x\) than \(y\)), shrinking as \(k\) increases toward \(4\).

    • \(k = 4\): just the origin (degenerate ellipse).

    • \(k > 4\): empty (no real trace).

  2. Find the vertical traces in the \(xz\)-plane (\(y = 0\)) and the \(yz\)-plane (\(x = 0\)).

    \(y = 0\): \(z = 4 - x^2\) — a downward parabola with vertex at \((0, 4)\).

    \(x = 0\): \(z = 4 - 4y^2\) — a downward parabola with vertex at \((0, 4)\), narrower than the first.

  3. Sketch the surface. Label the vertex and at least one trace in each direction.

    Elliptic paraboloid opening downward, with vertex at \((0, 0, 4)\) and axis of symmetry along the \(z\)-axis. The \(xz\)-trace (\(y=0\)) is a downward parabola \(z = 4-x^2\) with \(x\)-intercepts at \(\pm 2\). The \(yz\)-trace (\(x=0\)) is a narrower downward parabola \(z = 4-4y^2\) with \(y\)-intercepts at \(\pm 1\). Horizontal slices are ellipses that widen as \(z\) decreases from \(4\).

  4. Sketch a contour map (in the \(xy\)-plane) showing the level curves for \(z = 0\), \(z = 2\), and \(z = 4\). Label each curve with its \(z\)-value.

    Set \(z = k\): \(x^2 + 4y^2 = 4 - k\).

    \(z = 0\): \(x^2 + 4y^2 = 4\), i.e. \(\dfrac{x^2}{4} + y^2 = 1\) — ellipse with semi-axes \(a = 2\), \(b = 1\).

    \(z = 2\): \(x^2 + 4y^2 = 2\), i.e. \(\dfrac{x^2}{2} + \dfrac{y^2}{1/2} = 1\) — ellipse with semi-axes \(a = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.41\), \(b = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.71\).

    \(z = 4\): \(x^2 + 4y^2 = 0\) — just the origin (the vertex, viewed from above).

    The contour map shows concentric ellipses shrinking inward toward the origin as \(z\) increases. The outermost ellipse is \(z = 0\) and the innermost “curve” is the single point \(z = 4\).

Question 2. 6 pts

Consider the helix \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle 3\cos t,\, 3\sin t,\, 4t \rangle\).

  1. Find the first point (with \(t > 0\)) where the curve pierces the \(xz\)-plane.

    The \(xz\)-plane is where \(y = 0\), so \(3\sin t = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; t = n\pi\) for integer \(n\). The first positive value is \(t = \pi\). \[\mathbf{r}(\pi) = \langle 3\cos\pi,\, 3\sin\pi,\, 4\pi \rangle = \boxed{(-3,\, 0,\, 4\pi)}.\]

  2. Find the unit tangent vector \(\mathbf{T}\) at the point from (a).

    \(\mathbf{r}'(t) = \langle -3\sin t,\; 3\cos t,\; 4 \rangle.\)

    At \(t = \pi\): \(\mathbf{r}'(\pi) = \langle 0,\, -3,\, 4 \rangle\).

    \(|\mathbf{r}'(\pi)| = \sqrt{0 + 9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5.\)

    \[\boxed{\mathbf{T}(\pi) = \frac{1}{5}\langle 0,\, -3,\, 4 \rangle = \left\langle 0,\, -\frac{3}{5},\, \frac{4}{5} \right\rangle.}\]

  3. Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2} \mathbf{r}(t)\, dt\).

    \[\begin{aligned} \int_0^{\pi/2} \langle 3\cos t,\, 3\sin t,\, 4t \rangle\, dt &= \left\langle 3\sin t\Big|_0^{\pi/2},\; -3\cos t\Big|_0^{\pi/2},\; 2t^2\Big|_0^{\pi/2} \right\rangle \\ &= \left\langle 3 - 0,\; 0 - (-3),\; \frac{\pi^2}{2} - 0 \right\rangle = \boxed{\left\langle 3,\, 3,\, \frac{\pi^2}{2} \right\rangle}. \end{aligned}\]
