Question 1. 5 pts

Determine whether the lines \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) intersect. If they do, find the point of intersection. If they do not, determine whether they are parallel or skew. \[L_1:\; x = 1+t,\quad y = 2+2t,\quad z = -1+3t\] \[L_2:\; x = 3+s,\quad y = 6-s,\quad z = 5+s\]

Set components equal: \[\begin{aligned} 1 + t &= 3 + s &&\Longrightarrow\quad t - s = 2 \quad\text{(i)}\\ 2 + 2t &= 6 - s &&\Longrightarrow\quad 2t + s = 4 \quad\text{(ii)}\\ -1 + 3t &= 5 + s &&\Longrightarrow\quad 3t - s = 6 \quad\text{(iii)} \end{aligned}\]

From (i) + (ii): \(3t = 6\), so \(t = 2\) and \(s = 0\).

Check (iii): \(3(2) - 0 = 6\)

All three equations are satisfied, so the lines intersect at \[\bigl(1+2,\; 2+4,\; -1+6\bigr) = \boxed{(3, 6, 5)}.\]

Question 2. 5 pts

Find the distance from the point \(P(3,1,-2)\) to the plane \(2x - 2y + z = 6\).

The plane has normal vector \(\mathbf{n} = \langle 2,-2,1\rangle\).

Method 1 (normal line). The line through \(P\) perpendicular to the plane is \[x = 3+2t,\quad y = 1-2t,\quad z = -2+t.\] Substitute into \(2x - 2y + z = 6\): \[2(3+2t) - 2(1-2t) + (-2+t) = 6 \;\Longrightarrow\; 9t + 2 = 6 \;\Longrightarrow\; t = \tfrac{4}{9}.\] The foot of the perpendicular is \(F = \bigl(\tfrac{35}{9},\, \tfrac{1}{9},\, \tfrac{-14}{9}\bigr)\). \[d = |PF| = \sqrt{\bigl(\tfrac{8}{9}\bigr)^2 + \bigl(\tfrac{-8}{9}\bigr)^2 + \bigl(\tfrac{4}{9}\bigr)^2} = \frac{\sqrt{144}}{9} = \boxed{\frac{4}{3}}.\]

Method 2 (scalar projection). Choose any point on the plane, e.g. \(Q(0,0,6)\). \[\overrightarrow{QP} = \langle 3, 1, -8\rangle, \qquad |\mathbf{n}| = \sqrt{4+4+1} = 3.\] The distance is the absolute scalar projection of \(\overrightarrow{QP}\) onto \(\mathbf{n}\): \[d = \frac{|\overrightarrow{QP} \cdot \mathbf{n}|}{|\mathbf{n}|} = \frac{|6 - 2 - 8|}{3} = \boxed{\frac{4}{3}}.\]

Question 3. 5 pts

Find a vector equation for the line of intersection of the planes \[x + y - z = 2 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 2x - y + z = 1.\]

Direction vector. The line lies in both planes, so its direction is perpendicular to both normal vectors. With \(\mathbf{n}_1 = \langle 1,1,-1\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{n}_2 = \langle 2,-1,1\rangle\): \[\mathbf{n}_1 \times \mathbf{n}_2 = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ 1 & 1 & -1 \\ 2 & -1 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = \langle 0, -3, -3 \rangle.\] We can use \(\mathbf{d} = \langle 0, 1, 1\rangle\) (dividing by \(-3\)).

Point on the line. Set \(z = 0\) and solve: \[\begin{aligned} x + y &= 2 \\ 2x - y &= 1 \end{aligned}\] Adding: \(3x = 3\), so \(x = 1\), \(y = 1\). A point on the line is \((1, 1, 0)\).

Vector equation: \[\boxed{\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle 1, 1, 0\rangle + t\,\langle 0, 1, 1\rangle.}\]
