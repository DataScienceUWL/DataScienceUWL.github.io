Instructions. (15 points) Sections 16.7, 16.8, 16.9. Show relevant work for full credit.

Question 1 [3 pts]

Compute \(\displaystyle\iint_S z\,dS\) where \(S\) is the cylindrical surface \(x^2 + y^2 = 1\), \(0 \le z \le 2\).

Parametrize the cylinder by \(\mathbf{r}(\theta, z) = \langle\cos\theta,\; \sin\theta,\; z\rangle\) for \(0 \le \theta \le 2\pi\), \(0 \le z \le 2\). Then \[\mathbf{r}_\theta = \langle -\sin\theta,\, \cos\theta,\, 0\rangle, \quad \mathbf{r}_z = \langle 0,\, 0,\, 1\rangle, \quad \mathbf{r}_\theta\times\mathbf{r}_z = \langle\cos\theta,\, \sin\theta,\, 0\rangle,\] so \(|\mathbf{r}_\theta\times\mathbf{r}_z| = 1\). Therefore \[\iint_S z\,dS = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^2 z\cdot 1\,dz\,d\theta = 2\pi\cdot 2 = \boxed{4\pi}.\]

Question 2 [3 pts]

Compute \(\displaystyle\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}\) where \(\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = \langle 0,\; 0,\; z\rangle\) and \(S\) is the part of the paraboloid \(z = 4 - x^2 - y^2\) lying above the \(xy\)-plane, oriented upward.

The surface is the graph \(z = g(x,y) = 4 - x^2 - y^2\) over the disk \(D: x^2 + y^2 \le 4\). For an upward-oriented graph, \[d\mathbf{S} = \langle -g_x,\; -g_y,\; 1\rangle\,dA = \langle 2x,\; 2y,\; 1\rangle\,dA, \qquad \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = z\,dA = (4 - x^2 - y^2)\,dA.\] In polar: \[\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^2 (4 - r^2)\,r\,dr\,d\theta = 2\pi(8 - 4) = \boxed{8\pi}.\]

Question 3 [3 pts]

Let \(\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = \langle z^2,\; 2x,\; -y^3\rangle\) and let \(C\) be the unit circle \(x^2 + y^2 = 1\) in the plane \(z = 0\), oriented clockwise when viewed from above. Use Stokes’ Theorem to compute \(\displaystyle\oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}\).

Choose a surface and orient it. Take \(S\) to be the unit disk \(\{x^2+y^2\le 1,\, z=0\}\). Because \(C\) is traversed clockwise viewed from above, the right-hand rule forces the surface normal to point downward: \(d\mathbf{S} = \langle 0, 0, -1\rangle\,dA\).

Compute the curl. \[\nabla\times\mathbf{F} = \langle (-y^3)_y - (2x)_z,\; (z^2)_z - (-y^3)_x,\; (2x)_x - (z^2)_y\rangle = \langle -3y^2,\; 2z,\; 2\rangle.\] On \(S\) (\(z=0\)): \(\nabla\times\mathbf{F} = \langle -3y^2,\; 0,\; 2\rangle\).

Apply Stokes’. \[\oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S (\nabla\times\mathbf{F})\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iint_S (2)(-1)\,dA = -2\cdot\mathrm{Area}(S) = \boxed{-2\pi}.\]

Question 4 [3 pts]

Let \(\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = \langle xz^2,\; y^3,\; x^2 z\rangle\) and let \(S\) be the unit sphere \(x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1\) with outward orientation. Use the Divergence Theorem to compute \(\displaystyle\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}\).

\(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} = z^2 + 3y^2 + x^2\). With \(E\) the closed unit ball, \[\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \iiint_E (x^2 + 3y^2 + z^2)\,dV.\]

Symmetry shortcut. By the symmetry of the unit ball, \(\iiint_E x^2\,dV = \iiint_E y^2\,dV = \iiint_E z^2\,dV\). Their sum equals \[\iiint_E (x^2+y^2+z^2)\,dV = \iiint_E \rho^2\,dV = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\pi\!\!\int_0^1 \rho^4\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta = 2\pi\cdot 2\cdot\tfrac{1}{5} = \tfrac{4\pi}{5},\] so each integral equals \(\tfrac{1}{3}\cdot\tfrac{4\pi}{5} = \tfrac{4\pi}{15}\).

Therefore \[\iiint_E (x^2 + 3y^2 + z^2)\,dV = (1 + 3 + 1)\cdot\tfrac{4\pi}{15} = \boxed{\tfrac{4\pi}{3}}.\]

Question 5 [3 pts]

For each of the following, identify the single best method for evaluation: direct parametrization, FTLI, Green’s Theorem, Stokes’ Theorem, or the Divergence Theorem. Give a one-sentence reason. (You do not need to evaluate any of them.)

(a) \(\displaystyle\oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}\) where \(\mathbf{F} = \langle xy,\; e^{x^2},\; z\rangle\) and \(C\) is the triangle with vertices \((1,0,0)\), \((0,1,0)\), \((0,0,1)\) traversed once.

(b) \(\displaystyle\iint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}\) where \(\mathbf{F} = \langle x + e^y,\; y + \sin z,\; z\rangle\) and \(S\) is the boundary of the cube \([0,1]^3\) with outward orientation.

(c) \(\displaystyle\int_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}\) where \(\mathbf{F} = \langle 2x,\; 2y,\; 2z\rangle\) and \(C\) is any smooth path from \((0,0,0)\) to \((1,2,3)\).

(a) Stokes’ Theorem. \(C\) is a closed curve in 3D, and direct parametrization is awkward (three segments, integrand contains \(e^{x^2}\) which has no elementary antiderivative). Replace with the curl flux through the flat triangular surface \(S\) whose boundary is \(C\).

(b) Divergence Theorem. \(S\) is a closed surface bounding a solid, and \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3\) is simple, so \(\iiint_E 3\,dV = 3\cdot\mathrm{Vol} = 3\) is far easier than computing flux through six faces.

(c) FTLI. \(\mathbf{F} = \nabla(x^2 + y^2 + z^2)\) is conservative, so \(\int_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = f(1,2,3) - f(0,0,0) = 14\) no matter what \(C\) is.
