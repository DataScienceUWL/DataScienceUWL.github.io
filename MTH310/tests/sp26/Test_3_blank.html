Exam 3 MTH 310 — Sections 15.3--16.6

Instructions. (100 points) Sections 15.3--16.6. You have approximately 70 minutes. You may use a calculator. Show all work for credit. Several problems ask you to \textbf{set up but not evaluate}; for those, \emph{full credit will be given only if your setup uses a coordinate system that makes the integral easy to evaluate.} Setups that are correct but unnecessarily difficult will not earn full credit.

Question 1 [12 pts]

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\iint_D x\,y\;dA\), where \(D\) is the portion of the disk \(x^2 + y^2 \le 4\) lying in the first quadrant.

Question 2 [12 pts]

Set up, but do not evaluate, an integral for the surface area of the part of the upper hemisphere \(z = \sqrt{4 - x^2 - y^2}\) that lies inside the cylinder \(x^2 + y^2 = 1\). Full credit requires a setup whose integrals can be evaluated easily.

Question 3 [12 pts]

Set up, but do not evaluate, an integral for the volume of the solid bounded by the paraboloids \(y = x^2 + z^2\) and \(y = 4 - x^2 - z^2\). Full credit requires a setup whose integral can be evaluated easily.

Question 4 [12 pts]

Set up, but do not evaluate, an integral for the volume of the solid bounded above by the sphere \(x^2+y^2+z^2 = 4\) and below by the cone \(z = \sqrt{3(x^2+y^2)}\). Full credit requires a setup whose integral can be evaluated easily.

Question 5 [12 pts]

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}\) where \(\mathbf{F}(x,y) = \langle y,\; x + y\rangle\) and \(C\) is the parabolic arc \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle t,\, t^2\rangle\), \(0 \le t \le 1\).

Question 6 [14 pts]

Let \(\mathbf{F}(x,y) = \langle 2xy + y,\; x^2 + x\rangle\), and let \(C\) be the closed path from \((0,0)\) to \((1,1)\) along \(y = x^2\), then back from \((1,1)\) to \((0,0)\) along \(y = x\).

(a) (4 pts) Is \(\mathbf{F}\) conservative? If so, what does that imply about \(\displaystyle\oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}\) before doing any computation?

(b) (6 pts) Verify your answer in (a) by applying Green’s Theorem.

(c) (4 pts) Find a potential function \(f(x,y)\) with \(\nabla f = \mathbf{F}\).

Question 7 [14 pts]

Use Green’s Theorem with \(\mathbf{F}(x,y) = \left\langle -\tfrac{1}{2}\,y,\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,x\right\rangle\) to prove that the area enclosed by the ellipse \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} + \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\) is \(\pi a b\).

(a) (6 pts) Compute \(Q_x - P_y\) for this \(\mathbf{F}\). What does Green’s Theorem then tell you about the value of \(\displaystyle\oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}\) when \(C\) is any simple closed curve in the plane oriented counterclockwise?

(b) (8 pts) Parameterize the ellipse and compute \(\displaystyle\oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}\) directly. Use part (a) to conclude that the area enclosed by the ellipse is \(\pi a b\).

Question 8 [12 pts]

For each statement, decide whether it is true or false, and give one sentence of justification.

(a) (3 pts) If \(\mathbf{F}\) is continuously differentiable on \(\mathbb{R}^2\) and \(P_y = Q_x\) at every point, then \(\mathbf{F}\) is conservative.

(b) (3 pts) If \(\displaystyle\oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0\) for one particular simple closed curve \(C\), then \(\mathbf{F}\) is conservative.

(c) (3 pts) If \(\mathrm{div}\,\mathbf{F} = 0\) at every point of a region, then \(\displaystyle\oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0\) for every closed curve \(C\) in that region.

(d) (3 pts) Green’s Theorem applies whenever \(C\) is a simple closed curve in the plane and \(P,Q\) are continuously differentiable on the region \(D\) enclosed by \(C\).
