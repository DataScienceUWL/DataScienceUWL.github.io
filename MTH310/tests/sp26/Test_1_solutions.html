Question 1. 10 pts

Let \(A = (1, 3, -2)\), \(B = (4, 1, 2)\), and \(C = (2, 5, 0)\).

Find the scalar projection of \(\overrightarrow{AC}\) onto \(\overrightarrow{AB}\).

\(\overrightarrow{AB} = \langle 3, -2, 4 \rangle\), \(\overrightarrow{AC} = \langle 1, 2, 2 \rangle\)

\(\overrightarrow{AC} \cdot \overrightarrow{AB} = 3 - 4 + 8 = 7\)

\(|\overrightarrow{AB}| = \sqrt{9 + 4 + 16} = \sqrt{29}\)

\(\displaystyle\text{comp}_{\overrightarrow{AB}} \overrightarrow{AC} = \frac{\overrightarrow{AC} \cdot \overrightarrow{AB}}{|\overrightarrow{AB}|} = \frac{7}{\sqrt{29}}\)

Question 2. 12 pts

Consider the two lines: \[\begin{aligned} L_1&: \quad x = 2 + t, \quad y = 1 - t, \quad z = 3t \\ L_2&: \quad x = 4 + 2s, \quad y = -1 - 2s, \quad z = 6s \end{aligned}\]

Are the lines parallel, intersecting, or skew? Justify your answer completely:

  • If they are parallel, determine whether they are the same line or two distinct lines.

  • If they intersect, find the point of intersection.

Direction vectors: \(\mathbf{d_1} = \langle 1, -1, 3 \rangle\), \(\mathbf{d_2} = \langle 2, -2, 6 \rangle = 2\mathbf{d_1}\).

Since \(\mathbf{d_2}\) is a scalar multiple of \(\mathbf{d_1}\), the lines are parallel.

Now check if they are the same line. \(L_1\) passes through \((2, 1, 0)\) (set \(t = 0\)). \(L_2\) passes through \((4, -1, 0)\) (set \(s = 0\)).

Is \((4, -1, 0)\) on \(L_1\)? Solve \(2 + t = 4 \Rightarrow t = 2\). Then \(y = 1 - 2 = -1\) () and \(z = 6 \neq 0\) ().

Since \((4, -1, 0) \notin L_1\), the lines are parallel but distinct.

Question 3. 16 pts

Consider the surface \(4x^2 - y^2 + 4z^2 = 16\).

(a) Find the family of traces in the most important direction and at least one trace in each of the other two coordinate directions. Use these to give a complete description of the surface.

Divide by 16: \(\displaystyle\frac{x^2}{4} - \frac{y^2}{16} + \frac{z^2}{4} = 1\)

Trace \(y = k\) (most important — the family): \(\frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{z^2}{4} = 1 + \frac{k^2}{16}\) — circles of radius \(2\sqrt{1 + k^2/16}\) for all values of \(k\). The circles grow as \(|k|\) increases. The smallest circle is at \(y = 0\) with radius \(2\).

Trace \(z = 0\) (\(xy\)-plane): \(\frac{x^2}{4} - \frac{y^2}{16} = 1\) — hyperbola opening in the \(x\)-direction, vertices at \((\pm 2, 0, 0)\).

Trace \(x = 0\) (\(yz\)-plane): \(\frac{z^2}{4} - \frac{y^2}{16} = 1\) — hyperbola opening in the \(z\)-direction, vertices at \((0, 0, \pm 2)\).

This is a hyperboloid of one sheet with axis along the \(y\)-axis. The waist (smallest circle) is at \(y = 0\) with radius \(2\).

(b) Carefully sketch the surface, labeling the intercepts and the axis of symmetry.

Intercepts: \((\pm 2, 0, 0)\) and \((0, 0, \pm 2)\). No \(y\)-intercepts.

Axis of symmetry: the \(y\)-axis. The surface looks like a cooling tower with circular cross-sections, narrowest at \(y = 0\) and widening as \(|y|\) increases.

Circular traces at y equals plus or minus 4 (radius 2 times the square root of 2) are shown in red and green. A purple hyperbola in the xz-plane shows the trace z equals 0. Question 4. 16 pts

Let \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle 2\cos t,\; 2\sin t,\; 3t \rangle\).

(a) Show that the curve lies on a circular cylinder and give its equation.

\(x = 2\cos t\) and \(y = 2\sin t\), so \(x^2 + y^2 = 4\cos^2 t + 4\sin^2 t = 4\).

The curve lies on the cylinder \(x^2 + y^2 = 4\) (radius 2, axis along \(z\)).

(b) Find the unit tangent vector \(\mathbf{T}\) at \(t = \pi/2\).

\(\mathbf{r}\,'(t) = \langle -2\sin t,\; 2\cos t,\; 3 \rangle\)

\(|\mathbf{r}\,'(t)| = \sqrt{4\sin^2 t + 4\cos^2 t + 9} = \sqrt{4 + 9} = \sqrt{13}\)

\(\mathbf{r}\,'(\pi/2) = \langle -2, 0, 3 \rangle\)

\(\displaystyle\mathbf{T}(\pi/2) = \frac{\mathbf{r}\,'(\pi/2)}{|\mathbf{r}\,'(\pi/2)|} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{13}}\langle -2, 0, 3 \rangle = \left\langle \frac{-2}{\sqrt{13}},\; 0,\; \frac{3}{\sqrt{13}} \right\rangle\)

(c) Find parametric equations for the tangent line to the curve at \(t = \pi/2\).

The point is \(\mathbf{r}(\pi/2) = \langle 0, 2, 3\pi/2 \rangle\) and the direction is \(\mathbf{r}\,'(\pi/2) = \langle -2, 0, 3 \rangle\).

\(x = -2s, \quad y = 2, \quad z = \tfrac{3\pi}{2} + 3s\)

Question 5. 12 pts

Find a vector function \(\mathbf{r}(t)\) that represents the curve of intersection of the cylinder \(y^2 + z^2 = 4\) and the surface \(x = yz\).

The cylinder \(y^2 + z^2 = 4\) is parameterized by \(y = 2\cos t\), \(z = 2\sin t\).

Then \(x = yz = (2\cos t)(2\sin t) = 4\cos t \sin t = 2\sin 2t\).

\[\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle 2\sin 2t,\; 2\cos t,\; 2\sin t \rangle, \quad 0 \leq t \leq 2\pi\]

Question 6. 12 pts

Consider the limit: \[\lim_{(x,y) \to (0,0)} \frac{x\, y^4}{x^4 + y^4}\]

Does this limit exist or not exist? Provide careful evidence to support your conclusion. If it exists, prove it. If it does not exist, show why.

The limit exists and equals \(0\).

Since \(y^4 \leq x^4 + y^4\), we have \(\displaystyle\frac{y^4}{x^4 + y^4} \leq 1\).

Therefore: \[\left|\frac{x\, y^4}{x^4 + y^4}\right| = |x| \cdot \frac{y^4}{x^4 + y^4} \leq |x| \cdot 1 = |x|\]

As \((x,y) \to (0,0)\), \(|x| \to 0\), so by the Squeeze Theorem: \[\lim_{(x,y) \to (0,0)} \frac{x\, y^4}{x^4 + y^4} = 0\]

Question 7. 12 pts

For \(g(r,s,t) = e^r \sin(st)\), find the mixed partial derivative \(g_{rst}\).

\(g_r = e^r \sin(st)\)

\(g_{rs} = e^r \cdot t\cos(st)\)

\(g_{rst} = e^r\!\left[\cos(st) + t\bigl(-\sin(st)\bigr)\cdot s\right] = e^r\!\left[\cos(st) - st\sin(st)\right]\)

(Product rule on \(t\cos(st)\): derivative of \(t\) is \(1\), derivative of \(\cos(st)\) w.r.t. \(t\) is \(-s\sin(st)\).)

Question 8. 10 pts

The equation \(xz + yz^2 = 6\) defines \(z\) implicitly as a function of \(x\) and \(y\) near the point \((1, 1, 2)\).

Find \(\displaystyle\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}\) at \((1, 1, 2)\).

Differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\) (treating \(y\) as constant, \(z = z(x,y)\)):

\(z + x\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} + 2yz\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = 0\)

\(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}(x + 2yz) = -z \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = \frac{-z}{x + 2yz}\)

At \((1, 1, 2)\): \(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = \frac{-2}{1 + 2(1)(2)} = \frac{-2}{5}\)
